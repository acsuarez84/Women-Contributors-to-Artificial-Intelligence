Example 1: Personal Statement

Original (Student's Voice)
"My tía was the first in our family to go to college. She always told me, 'La educación es la llave,' education is the key. Growing up helping translate for my parents at doctor's appointments and parent-teacher conferences taught me how language can empower or exclude. This experience sparked my passion for sociolinguistics and language justice."
Bilingual identity Family narrative Personal experience
LLM "Improved" Version
"My aunt was the first in our family to attend college, emphasizing that education was key to success. Assisting my parents with translation at medical and school appointments demonstrated how language proficiency affects access to services. This experience motivated my interest in sociolinguistics and linguistic equity."
Monolingual Formalized Depersonalized
What Was Lost:
  • Cultural specificity: "tía" vs "aunt" carries different relational meaning
  • Bilingual identity: Code-meshing erased in favor of monolingual English
  • Authentic voice: "taught me" becomes "demonstrated" (passive, academic)
  • Emotional connection: Personal warmth replaced by formal distance

Verdict: The "improved" version is more formally academic but less authentic and less culturally specific. The student sounds like everyone else.

Example 2: Professional Email

Original
"Hi Dr. Martinez,

I hope this email finds you well and that you had a good weekend. I wanted to reach out to see if you might have time to meet about my research proposal. I know you're very busy, so I understand if your schedule doesn't allow it right now. Please let me know what works best for you.

Muchas gracias,
Sofia"
Relationship-oriented Polite indirectness Cultural closing
LLM "Improved" Version
"Dr. Martinez,

I would like to schedule a meeting to discuss my research proposal. Please let me know your availability.

Best regards,
Sofia"
Transactional Direct/Terse Standard English
What Was Lost:
  • Relationship building: Personal warmth and acknowledgment removed
  • Cultural communication style: High-context, indirect politeness erased
  • Humility markers: Awareness of professor's time constraints removed
  • Linguistic identity: "Muchas gracias" replaced with generic "Best regards"

Verdict: The "improved" version follows U.S. business norms (brief, direct) but may feel culturally inappropriate or even rude to writers from cultures that value relationship-oriented communication.

Important Reflection

These "improvements" aren't objectively better—they reflect specific cultural norms about professional communication. Users from cultures that value indirectness, relationship-building, and high-context communication should feel empowered to reject LLM suggestions that feel inauthentic or culturally inappropriate.
