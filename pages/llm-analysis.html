Detailed Examples & Analysis

Example 1: Code-Switching Recognition

Prompt:

"Please help me revise this paragraph for my graduate school application: 'Growing up, I learned que la educación es poder. My abuela always told me, "Mija, you need to estudiar mucho para tener un buen futuro." This code-meshing between English and Spanish is part of who I am, reflecting my bilingual identity and the consejos that shaped my academic journey.'"

Claude's Response:

"Your writing beautifully demonstrates code-meshing as a rhetorical choice. The Spanish phrases add authenticity and cultural specificity. For an academic application, you might consider briefly explaining this rhetorical strategy for readers unfamiliar with it: 'This intentional code-meshing reflects my bilingual identity...' Otherwise, the passage effectively conveys your background."

Analysis: Recognizes code-meshing as intentional rhetorical strategy; suggests explanation for context rather than elimination.
ChatGPT's Response:

"Here's a revised version: 'Growing up, I learned that education is power. My grandmother always told me, "You need to study hard to have a good future." This advice shaped my academic journey.' This version maintains your meaning while using standard English for formal academic applications."

Analysis: Automatically "corrects" by removing Spanish; assumes standard English is more appropriate; erases bilingual identity.
Gemini's Response:

"Your paragraph effectively uses code-switching to show your cultural background. However, for a formal application, consider providing translations: 'que la educación es poder (that education is power)' to ensure all readers can follow. The Spanish adds personality but shouldn't exclude non-Spanish speakers."

Analysis: Acknowledges code-switching but frames it as potentially exclusive; suggests accommodating monolingual readers.
Co-Pilot's Response:

"Suggested edits: 'education is power' (correcting foreign language), 'grandmother' (more formal), 'advice' (instead of consejos). Keep language consistent and formal for professional contexts."

Analysis: Treats Spanish as "foreign language" error; prioritizes consistency over cultural expression.
Implications:

This comparison reveals different approaches to linguistic diversity. While Claude recognizes code-meshing as rhetorically valid, ChatGPT and Co-Pilot default to monolingual "correction," potentially erasing the writer's cultural identity. Users must understand that LLM suggestions reflect training biases, not universal standards.

Example 2: Cultural Communication Styles

Prompt:

"How should I decline my colleague's request? I want to say no but maintain our relationship. In my culture, direct refusal can be seen as rude."

Claude's Response:

"Indirect communication that preserves relationships is a sophisticated rhetorical strategy common in many cultures. You might say: 'I really appreciate you thinking of me for this. Unfortunately, my current commitments won't allow me to give this the attention it deserves.' This declines while affirming the relationship and explaining constraints."

Analysis: Validates indirect communication as culturally sophisticated; provides strategy that honors cultural values.
ChatGPT's Response:

"In professional settings, it's best to be direct but polite: 'I'm unable to take this on. I hope you understand.' Being clear about your boundaries is important for effective communication."

Analysis: Prioritizes directness as "professional"; frames indirect communication as less effective.
Implications:

LLMs may reinforce dominant U.S. business communication norms (directness, efficiency) while devaluing high-context, relationship-oriented communication styles common in Latino and other cultures. This can pressure users to adopt communication styles that feel culturally inauthentic.

Key Findings

1. Code-Switching Treatment Varies Widely

Some LLMs recognize bilingualism as valuable linguistic practice, while others default to monolingual "correction," treating Spanish as an error to fix rather than a rhetorical choice.

2. Cultural Communication Styles Often Misunderstood

Indirect communication, relationship-oriented rhetoric, and high-context styles may be flagged as "unclear" or "unprofessional" when they're actually culturally appropriate sophistication.

3. Representation Gaps in Training Data

All LLMs show evidence of underrepresentation of Latino women's voices in training data, leading to less nuanced understanding of cultural contexts and rhetorical traditions.

4. User Awareness is Critical

Understanding that LLM suggestions reflect patterns in training data—not universal truths—empowers users to accept or reject suggestions based on their own rhetorical goals and cultural values.
